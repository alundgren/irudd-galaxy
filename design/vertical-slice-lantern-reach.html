Vertical Slice Proposal — The Lantern Reach

A proposal for one coherent, playable vertical slice of the mining and trading game, about 20 times the size of one disposable prototype: three regions, six stations, three factions, and the arc from a hands-on first ship to first automation and a first prestige choice, with the game master changing the world live. It builds on the High-Level Plan (source of truth), Keeping a Living Galaxy New and The Game Master's Menu. All pages are listed on Home.

The proposal in one card

Recommendation Build Shape A, the Lantern Reach: one full arc in a small living region.

Twenty systems, each about one prototype's worth, carry the player from a Tier 0 Wren flown with WASD and Space, through every control unlock and a first automated craft, to the Hearthbound choice between Tandem and Heron. Around that arc, three factions and a live game master change places and people through validated scripts, and a thin fast-model layer on OpenRouter makes traders and docks react moment to moment. It is a native desktop app that always assumes internet; authored goals and places form the journey the game master builds on. The code stays disposable; the design is representative.

What the game master does live

It works only through the scripting engine. Every change passes validation, carries a recorded cause, appears in a place and a person, and can be rolled back in-world. Authored goals and places carry the journey; the game master builds on them.

1
Grow a station from deliveriesKettle Dock's greenhouse gains visible stages as the player brings ice and plate; new stock appears on its market.
2
Run a shortage that reacts to historyHeavy ice mining in the Shallows starts an ice shortage touching three stations, with two ways to help and a resolution if ignored.
3
Move faction territoryThe Cinder Hook dispute ends with a depot changing hands, shown on the map and in station art, with the cause recorded.
4
Plant a rumor with a truth valueA dockhand mentions a rich glassite vein; it may be real, or a Compact plant to empty a belt.
5
Add a new verb with a teacherA named NPC introduces the first automated craft, or rock tagging, and uses it in front of the player first.
6
Open an Ancients chapterThe fragment from a rock the player mined opens chapter one at Glass Drift, inside the canon locks.
7
Create the sixth stationA station type nobody designed in advance, placed near something the player did, assembled from the runtime asset kit.
8
Write the chronicleEvery change becomes a chronicle entry and something the dockmaster can tell you when you ask what changed.

What the fast-model layer does

Proposed Thin, OpenRouter only, always online

  • • NPC traders choose routes and cargo from a menu the world allows, so prices move for reasons other than the player.
  • • Dock talk picks or phrases a line only when it can cite a real fact in history; otherwise nothing is said.
  • • Rumors spread from station to station by NPC choices, carrying their truth value with them.
  • • Outcomes are written back to history, where the game master reads them.
  • • Runs only on OpenRouter (small LLMs, or decision models such as TypeSafe Jev), inside a budget the game master sets; no local model. A failed or slow call is retried once or that moment is skipped.

In and out

In the slice

  • + Calm, solo, top-down, Star Traders sketched warmth
  • + Arcade WASD that stops on release, real pause
  • + Controls as progression up to queues and loops
  • + Scouted living prices with saturation
  • + One automated craft, one prestige choice
  • + Live game master with validation and rollback
  • + Local save on one computer, native desktop app, always online

Out of the slice

  • − Combat, pirates as fights, PvP, multiplayer
  • − Timers, countdowns, progress bars, days-left
  • − An approval inbox or feedback buttons in the game
  • − Cards as the core content format
  • − Player-built bases and stations
  • − A second prestige, Mammoth, Firefly or Oracle hulls
  • − A full talent tree or XP bars
  • − Live sync, Mac packaging, the rejected mining and engine sounds
  • − Filler flavor text

The player's journey, beat by beat

What Andreas sees and does, in order. Green tags mark where a new control or system unlocks.

Act 1 — Hands on · Tier 0: WASD plus Space

1
Undock the Wren
You leave Lantern Wharf in a small, sketched Wren. WASD flies, the ship stops when you let go, Space does the one thing in reach. Dockmaster Ilse asks for ice for the Kettle Dock greenhouse. One goal line, nothing else on screen.
2
Mine by hand
You park beside an ice rock and hold Space. The rock visibly shrinks and cracks; your hold fills. Different rocks look and behave differently.
3
First delivery
You drift into Kettle Dock's ring and press Space to dock. The greenhouse's first stage appears on the station itself, and stays there.
4
First sale and first upgrade
The next goal is a mining lens. You sell iron at the Wharf and watch the price dip a little with each unit you sell.
5
Pause whenever you like
Press P or Escape and the whole galaxy stops with an obvious PAUSED card. Nothing ever counts down.

Act 2 — Reach out · Click to fly, click to mine, click to dock, map click

6
The nav chipUnlocks: Click to fly, mine, dock
The hullwright fits your first nav chip: short click-to-fly hops. Click-to-mine and click-to-dock follow as goals are met.
7
Into Cinder DriftUnlocks: Map click travel
Ilse sends plate to the Cinder Hook camp. On the map, prices there stay unknown until you visit, then show as a snapshot dated by your last visit.
8
Chasing a gap
Brassmouth pays well for glassite until you flood it. You learn to split loads and scout ahead. The Tollway Compact starts to remember your name.
9
The Cinder Hook dispute
Cooperative and Compact both want the camp's ore. You choose whom to supply, in your own time. The depot changes hands on the map and both factions remember.
10
A rumor
A dockhand mentions a rich vein in the far belt. Following it pays off, or teaches you who planted it.

Act 3 — Let go · Queues and loops

11
Orders in a rowUnlocks: Queues, then loops
Shift-click chains fly, mine, dock and sell. Then you save a loop route between two stations you trust.
12
A shortage you caused
Your ice runs drained the Shallows. Kettle Dock, Lantern Wharf and Cinder Hook feel it. Two ways to help appear in conversation; if you ignore it, the world resolves it another way.
13
Your first automated craftUnlocks: First automation
A named NPC shows you the first automated craft working. You hand it your saved loop with the same verbs you use. It keeps working while you fly elsewhere.
14
Your pilot changes
Because of something you did many times, your pilot earns a trait, such as seeing a rock's richest face. It changes how you play a little, never how strong you are a lot.

Act 4 — A calling · First prestige choice

15
The fragment
A rock you mined held an Ancients fragment. The Lamplighters at Glass Drift ask you to carry it, and chapter one opens.
16
The hullwright's questionUnlocks: Hearthbound branch
Back at Lantern Wharf you can trade the Wren up into a Tandem (hauler that pairs with automation) or a Heron (scout with longer price reach). World, credits, standing and depletion persist; hull ranks reset for a fee.
17
Coming home
You ask Ilse what changed while you were away. She tells you, in her words. The sixth station is on the map, and the chronicle now has your name in it. The slice ends; the world keeps running.

Base engine hooks for the game master

The smallest surface the release-only base engine must expose so all eight live change types are scripts. Anything not on this list is out of the game master's reach in the slice.

Hook or store Used in the slice to Minimum
Event hooks React to dock, undock, mine, rock depleted, buy, sell, scout, order done, quest step, trait earned, prestige. Subscribe from a script; payload carries stable IDs.
Activity counters Pace beats by dockings, deliveries, jumps and sessions, never clock time. Read-only counters per player, station and region.
World state stores Change stations and build stages, markets, factions, territory, NPCs and rumors. Typed reads and writes, each write tagged with a cause.
Player history Find what the player did and build consequences on it. Append-only facts with simple queries.
Canon graph Keep Ancients chapter one consistent and guarded. A small graph of locked canon nodes that scripts cite.
Market levers Run shortages and new stock without touching price code. Add or scale producers and consumers per station.
Mechanic registry Introduce a new verb with a teacher. Per-save flags plus reserved input and UI slots.
Order verb API Let scripted NPC craft and the automated craft use the player's verbs. Issue fly, mine, dock, buy, sell, wait, loop.
Runtime asset kit Grow Kettle Dock and assemble the sixth station. Modular station parts, stages and color variants by ID.
Beat API Add state-driven beats and directed goals. Register a beat with preconditions and an end state.
Legibility channels Make every change seen: dock talk, map notes, chronicle. Each line must reference a history fact ID.
Fast-model budget Set what fast models may decide, and how often. Menus of allowed choices plus a budget per region.
Sandbox and snapshots Validate and roll back. Fork a save, fast-forward headless, compare, restore.
Telemetry Spot exploits and runaway prices. Economy and activity metrics per region.

Engine Validation every script passes, cut down for the slice

  • ✓ Types and schema: the script only touches declared hooks and state.
  • ✓ Canon check against the small canon graph.
  • ✓ Invariants: no timers, no level scaling, no destroyed player property, every shock paired with an opportunity.
  • ✓ Sandbox run: fork the current save, fast-forward headless, check prices and dead ends.
  • ✓ Legibility check: names a place and a person where it will be noticed.
  • ✓ Snapshot, then apply at a calm moment (docked or paused); roll back in-world if it misbehaves.

Splitting it across coding agents

Six workstreams, one T3 Code agent each (Opus or Sol), working from frozen contracts. Size is counted in systems and content, never in time.

  • • World-state schema with stable IDs for every station, rock, ship, NPC, faction and fact.
  • • Event names and payloads, including the activity counters.
  • • Order verb schema shared by the player ship, automated craft and scripted NPCs.
  • • Hook API surface the scripting engine may call (the table above), with nothing else reachable.
  • • Script manifest and validation report format, including the invariants.
  • • Asset manifest: sprite slots, station stages and variants by ID.
  • • Text channel API where every line carries the history fact it cites.

Working rule Rules for the agents

Each agent may only touch its own systems and the contract through a change request to W1. Stubs stay runnable at every merge. The dev harness's bot run of the whole journey is the shared test. Disposable code is fine; a broken contract is not.
