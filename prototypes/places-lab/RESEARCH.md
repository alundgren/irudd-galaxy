# places-lab research

How well-received games make a hub feel like a place you are in, move you between
places in it, and show people talking. Gathered from the web on 2026-11-01 and checked
against the pages cited. Each item names a technique; the games are references, not
things to rebuild. The lab builds our own station, people and art.

"Our reading" marks inference with no source behind it.

## People live in places

- **Inner lives.** Night in the Woods: "The characters need to have inner lives that have
  nothing to do with the player or even sometimes moving the plot along", and the town is
  "a place that felt lived in, where people were walking around, having lives".
  [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/road-to-the-igf-infinite-fall-s-i-night-in-the-woods-i-)
- **Small fixed details.** Same source: details like the house with too many Christmas
  decorations are things that "if they were ever missing you'd immediately feel like
  something about that place was off."
- **Homes for people.** Spiritfarer keeps a house for each spirit on the boat. The team
  wanted them removed as "dead weight"; the director kept them. Tone is "only 10-15%
  dramatic as opposed to 85-90% whimsical". [Pocket Tactics](https://www.pockettactics.com/spiritfarer/interview)
- **Where people eat says who they are.** Pentiment's meals were "for communicating
  social status and class": the table, the food, how it is served. [Origin Story](https://www.originstory.show/episodes/josh-sawyer)
- **Cast arcs.** Citizen Sleeper: "Over a season you have a set of character arcs ...
  have the player decide which characters appear." [Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/gaming/citizen-sleeper-gareth-martin-interview)

## Going between places

- **Scene change as stagecraft.** Kentucky Route Zero: "Moving between spaces recalls the
  scene changes of a play, with the fabric and architecture of the previous scene
  dissolving and falling away as new layers move resolutely into the light." A scene can
  also shift "the profile of the existing space ... even as it invites you in closer".
  [Bullet Points Monthly](https://bulletpointsmonthly.com/2020/03/18/theatrical-spaces/),
  [GDC 2014 session](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1020596/scenography-of-kentucky-route)
- **The cut that breaks the game in two.** Sunless Sea reviewer on docking: "you're
  ripped from the comfortable control scheme to read about adventures with NPC locals",
  giving "the impression that you're playing two games simultaneously". This is our
  ui-lab cradle problem. [Escapist](https://www.escapistmagazine.com/sunless-sea-review-horror-and-adventure-on-fallen-londons-unterzee/)
- **Home port.** Sunless Sea was shaped so you "feel relief when you saw London's lights
  in the distance"; the ship stayed slow because speed would reduce "the sense of space
  and distance". [Postmortem](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/postmortem-failbetter-games-i-sunless-sea-i-)
- **Rooms joined by doors.** Wing Commander's carrier: a rec room with the bartender and
  pilots, a door to the barracks, a hallway to the briefing room. Beds are save slots: "a
  person appears in that bed". [LP Archive](https://lparchive.org/Wing-Commander)
- **Still rooms with people to click.** Freelancer's stations are still-image locations
  with the bar as the centre; you click a person in the scene to talk.
  [Gameswelt (German)](https://www.gameswelt.de/freelancer/test/freelancer-4009)
- **The honest menu.** Star Wars: Squadrons' hangar is "menu-focused", with "a
  post-processing filter that would overlay ships while diving into menus"; free movement
  "fought against the focus". [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/marketing/what-star-wars-squadrons-can-teach-us-about-reviving-classic-genres)
- **A map you scroll is not a place.** Citizen Sleeper's travel by scrolling "doesn't at
  all simulate the feeling of navigating such an interesting world"; its designer: "I
  never get to put people inside a room." [RPGFan](https://www.rpgfan.com/review/citizen-sleeper/),
  [Creative Bloq via Yahoo](https://tech.yahoo.com/gaming/articles/game-development-more-accessible-ever-070000740.html)
- **Detail at every distance.** Same designer: an early, more detailed station "just
  didn't work at different scales"; zoomed in it needs "enough detail that it doesn't feel
  flat or empty".
- **Walking costs.** Fire Emblem: Three Houses' walkable monastery is called monotonous by
  players (mostly player opinion). [VGC](https://videogameschronicle.com/review/fire-emblem-three-houses)
  One Elite Dangerous player: the concourse "is always the same for the same kind of
  station ... it feels samey". [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/359320/discussions/0/4343229179786607162/)

## People talking

- **The camera on the speaker.** Mass Effect's close over-the-shoulder conversation
  camera, long lenses on faces rather than characters framed against backgrounds, and no
  black bars, "to blur the line between interactive dialogue and cutscenes". Procedural
  framing gave a baseline; "~70% were touched by hand". [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/10-facts-about-mass-effects-animation/)
- **Still portraits, events in text.** Citizen Sleeper: conversations with characters
  "represented as beautifully drawn still images—while the story's events are told in
  text". Wing Commander: "unvoiced talking heads". Both above.
- **A text column.** Disco Elysium's dialogue runs as a column modelled on Twitter, so
  "the text tumbles up". [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/disco-elysium-working-on-ui-design)
- **Sound gives the room.** Citizen Sleeper's areas "have their own ambient background
  noise ... the clanks of glassware and background chatter of a bar". [RPGFan](https://www.rpgfan.com/review/citizen-sleeper/)

## Coming back

- **Don't dump the place on arrival.** 80 Days: "Players - ourselves - were skipping
  through city content without reading it; the market felt like a chore", so exploring a
  city became optional. [Postmortem](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/postmortem-inkle-s-i-80-days-i-)
- **A hub that talks back.** Hades greets each return with new, fully voiced lines.
  [GDC](https://gdconf.com/article/dive-into-the-dialogue-of-hades-at-gdc-2021/) How Hades
  places characters in rooms, and Mass Effect: Andromeda's crew moving around the ship,
  were not verified.

## Lessons (our reading)

- Where the player and people are is game state; the camera and screens present it.
- Every way into a room should be one grammar, so the office and the cradle match.
- A cut needs a reason; a glide alone still reads as a cut if the room doesn't visibly
  belong to the module clicked.
- Speech belongs to a person's figure or face, never to a building.
- Walking alone doesn't make a place, and costs time on every visit.
