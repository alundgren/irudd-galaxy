Takeaway

Recommendation Keep art in code, make it richer, and let the game master write recipes, not assets

The parametric kit is already the right backbone: it is consistent by construction, every piece is ours under MIT, coding agents such as Grok Build can extend it and check their work in headless screenshots, and it is the only route that lets the game master build a station at runtime. What it lacks is richness: inner ink lines, decals and banners, more part kinds with faction flavour, and an art bible that the validator can enforce. Add Blender and glTF later for a few hero parts, use AI image models only for a 2D layer such as portraits and concept sheets, and consider commissioning one style sheet to anchor everything.

For audio, start from rules rather than sounds: no loops tied to held keys, information-bearing cues only, variation on everything that repeats, and silence as a valid state. Build a small cue set by synthesis rendered offline plus CC0 samples, routed through kira buses with ducking and region layers. The game master never ships raw meshes, images or audio into the game: it writes validated recipes and parameter sets over kits the base engine owns, and the base engine renders, checks and caches the result.

What the renderer and audio do today

The slice's renderer (crates/render) is a small wgpu 3D scene renderer with a top-down camera that can also tilt or go fully perspective. All art is generated in code by render::kit. The audio crate on the play branch wraps kira and plays nothing, by decision.

Area What exists Gap that matters for the art direction
Shading Cel shading with 2 or 3 hard tone bands from a half-Lambert key light, warm violet shadow multiplier (never gray), highlight band, hard rim light, stepped point-light pools, hatching in the shadow band, self-lit vertex paint and an emissive term. Set per instance through Material. Band thresholds are fixed in the shader, so every material bands the same way. No per-vertex control of where shadow falls, which is how stylised games keep faces and panels clean.
Outlines Inverted-hull ink outlines pushed out a constant number of pixels (default 4) along normals averaged across hard edges, so lines keep their weight at any zoom and under perspective. Only the outer silhouette gets a line. Creases, panel seams and colour borders inside a shape have none, so faceted rocks and stations read as flat colour steps. Inner lines are what make the look feel drawn.
Surface colour Flat vertex paint times a tint. Palettes are slots (hull, trim, accent, glow, glass, leaf) on a station recipe; palette.rs holds about twenty warm swatches. No UVs or textures, so no decals, faction marks, stripes, signage, banners or name plates. Faction identity can only come from colour today.
Parts Hulls from HullParams (Wren, Tandem, Heron); rocks of three kinds that shrink, chip and crack; stations as serde recipes of eight part kinds (hub, docking_ring, arm, greenhouse, antenna, tank, sail, lantern) with validate(), a 48-part cap and a 120-unit reach limit. Eight parts give few silhouettes: Lantern Wharf and Brassmouth are both wheels with spokes. No banner, spire, dome, hangar, crane or sign part, and no faction variants of a part.
Scene extras Gradient backdrop with nebula puffs and parallax stars; dashed, wobbly line ribbons; additive beams and glows; SDF panels and Fredoka text; the PAUSED card. No planets, no engine trails or small particles, almost no idle motion (blinking tips and beam shimmer only).
Checking cargo run -p render --example shot renders headless PNGs, including --gallery with every kit piece labelled. Generators are deterministic for a seed and unit tested. Validation covers numbers and reach only: no triangle budget, palette rule or silhouette check yet.
Contract and hooks contract::assets::AssetManifest is a placeholder: parts with an ID, name, free-form kind, optional asset ID and variant names. Luau scripts can attach and detach a part by ID, set a station's stage and owner. No hook to define a new recipe, palette, decal or sound; and the recipe types and their validator live in render, which the sim crates may not depend on.
Audio galaxy-audio opens a kira 0.12 manager on the default device, falls back to silence if none opens, and plays nothing. No buses, cues, ambience or music. The rejected mining-beam and engine loops are gone and stay gone.

What already works

The screenshots show a coherent look: warm cream, coral and teal against a violet sky, chunky ink lines, hatched shadows, glowing lanterns. Greenhouse stages and shrinking rocks already show change in the world, which is what the game master needs.

What reads as placeholder

Rocks dominate the mining view and look alike; the Wren is small next to them. Stations have no signs of who lives there. Surfaces are clean colour with no drawn detail, so the "illustrated" feel stops at the outline.

The art direction made concrete

One sentence to test every asset against

A warm, hand-inked storybook of frontier space: chunky low-poly shapes with thick ink lines, two or three flat tones, a few saturated glows, and every place telling you at a glance who built it and what it is for.

The four influences

Each influence contributes something specific. Each also has a part to leave behind, because copied wholesale they pull away from a calm, readable top-down game.

Influence Take How it shows up here Leave behind

Each faction owns a colour pair, a mark and a shape motif, so a station's owner reads from across the map. The Compact's brass and red counting-houses feel more ornate than the Cooperative's hand-built greenhouses. Grime, corporate satire everywhere, and ad copy on every wall. Palette rules

  • The sky is coloured, the ink is not black

    Space runs from #2a1f48 to #4e3672 with rose, amber, teal and violet nebulae. Lines and text use ink #2a1a2e. Shadows multiply toward warm violet. No pure black, no neutral gray, no blue-gray chrome (rejected in Prototype 4).

  • Three hues per object, in a 60/30/10 split

    Hull about 60 percent, trim about 30, accent about 10, plus ink and one glow colour. More hues than that and the object stops reading as one thing at top-down zoom.

  • Glow is reserved

    Lantern yellow, window cream and the beam teal mean "lit, alive or interactive". Hull paint never uses them at full strength, so the eye finds lights, docking rings and the mining beam first.

  • Value before hue

    Every hull must be clearly lighter than the sky behind it. Measured in OKLab lightness (a perceptual colour space where equal steps look equal), a hull should stay at least 0.25 above the backdrop's lightness. Rocks may sit darker than stations; ships always sit lighter than rocks.

  • Factions own colour pairs

    From the content spec: Wharf Cooperative, lantern green with a lantern-over-kettle mark; Tollway Compact, brass and deep red with a toll key; Lamplighter Society, amber on midnight blue with an open lamp. Each faction also gets a shape motif (below), so identity never rests on hue alone, which matters for colour-blind players.

  • Regions tint, they don't recolour

    The Shallows lean cool and minty, Cinder Drift warm and ember-orange, Brassmouth Narrows gold and plum. Region tint goes into the backdrop and point lights, never into an object's own paint.

Shape language and silhouettes

Shapes

  • Chunky and slightly oversized: thick struts, fat tanks, rounded caps. Nothing thinner than one world unit unless it ends in a glowing tip.
  • Big, medium, small: one dominant mass, a few secondary parts, a sprinkle of small detail.
  • A little lopsided on purpose: hand-built places are not perfectly symmetric.
  • Faction motifs: Cooperative round and organic (domes, greenhouses, patched plates); Compact square and ordered (arches, counting-house blocks, brass rings); Lamplighters tall and pointed (spires, lamp masts, open frames).

Readability at play zoom

  • A comfortable view is 80 to 120 units tall. At 720 pixels that is about 6 to 9 pixels per unit, so a Wren is roughly 40 to 55 pixels long and a 4-pixel outline eats half a unit on each side.
  • Every station kind needs its own top-down silhouette: wheel, cluster, spire with rings, dumbbell, crescent. Two wheels in one region is a failure.
  • A ship must be identifiable as a black shape at 32 pixels: nose, wings and engines in distinct positions.
  • Keep side and underside detail, since true-3D views of stations and planets are likely later.

Outlines and cel bands

Rule Value Why
Silhouette line Stations 4 to 5 px, ships 3 to 4 px, rocks 3 px, small props 2 to 3 px, in logical pixels, constant at any zoom. Weight shows importance. The renderer already holds the line at a constant pixel width.
Inner lines Ink at creases sharper than about 40 degrees and at borders between paint slots, at about half the silhouette width. The inverted hull can't draw inner lines; cel-shaded 3D fighting games solve this separately. The kit knows its hard edges when it builds a mesh, so it can emit them as line ribbons.
Line colour Ink everywhere; glass and glowing parts may use a darker shade of their own colour. A coloured line keeps lights soft instead of caged.
Bands Three bands by default (shadow, mid, highlight). Highlight covers less than a fifth of a lit object; shadow covers at least a quarter. Form reads from above only if the fixed sun leaves a real shadow side.
Hatching Shadow band only. Strong on rocks and rough plating, light on hulls, none on glass, glow or UI. Hatching is the "sketched" signature; on everything it turns into noise.
Rim light Kept hard and thin. Separates shapes from a coloured sky without outline-only contrast.
Shadow control Add a per-vertex "shadow bias" channel later, so a part can be told to stay lit or stay shaded. Some cel-shaded 3D games store shading control in vertex data rather than textures because it is resolution independent; it suits a texture-free kit.

Producing quality art

Four routes, judged on what matters for this project: the quality ceiling, how consistent the results stay, the effort, the licence position (the repo is public and MIT, and third-party material must be well known, reasonably safe and MIT-compatible), how well coding agents including Grok Build can do the work, and whether it carries over to true-3D views.

Four routes compared

An agent edits a generator, runs shot --gallery and looks at the result. Already real 3D meshes; needs more side and underside detail.

Ownership caution

The US Copyright Office concluded in January 2025 that prompts alone do not give enough human control to make the user the author of AI output, while AI used as a tool inside human work does not block protection. So a purely generated image may be usable under a tool's terms yet not protectable as ours. Art that matters to the game's identity should be authored, by code, a person, or heavy human editing.

Recommendation and first steps

Route (a) as the backbone, (b) for hero parts, (c) for a 2D layer, (d) to anchor the style

The kit is the only route the game master can use live, it is the most consistent, and it is the one agents can verify on their own by rendering and looking. Make it richer before adding anything else. Blender and glTF earn their place for a few hero parts that code can't shape well, plugged into recipes through named sockets. AI images suit portraits, station-screen headers and concept sheets, where a quantise-to-palette and ink pass keeps them on style. A single commissioned style sheet would give the agents and the validator a target better than any written rule.

  1. 1
    Write the art bible as data

    A palette file with roles (sky, ink, hull, trim, accent, glow) and faction and region pairs, plus the numeric rules on this page (hue count, lightness gap, line widths, minimum feature size). The validator and the agents read the same file.

  2. 2
    Add inner ink lines

    Have each kit generator emit its hard edges and paint-slot borders as line ribbons drawn with the outline colour. This is the biggest single step toward the drawn look.

  3. 3
    Add decals and banners

    A small decal kit of vector shapes (faction marks, stripes, chevrons, numbers) drawn as signed-distance quads laid on a part, and a banner part: a cloth-like quad that carries a mark. Signed distance keeps edges crisp at any zoom without textures.

  4. 4
    Grow the part kit with faction flavour

    From eight kinds toward about twenty: spire, dome, hangar, crane, arch, counting block, lamp mast, pod cluster, cargo rack, solar petals, beacon, scaffold. Give each a faction variant through shape parameters, not new code per faction.

  5. 5
    Build the contact-sheet loop for agents

    Extend shot with a sheet that shows each asset at play zoom, as a 64-pixel thumbnail, as a black silhouette, and next to its neighbours. Grok Build changes a generator, renders the sheet, compares it with the art bible and iterates; a reviewer agent on another model looks at the same PNGs.

  6. 6
    Spike one Blender part through glTF

    Agree a convention: paint slots in COLOR_0, sockets as named empties, line width and band count as custom properties exported to glTF extras, and a triangle budget. Load it with the gltf crate and check that it sits beside kit parts without looking foreign.

  7. 7
    Test a portrait and banner style

    Pick one generator with a clean licence, a fixed style prompt and reference sheet, and a post-process that maps the image onto the game palette and adds ink. Judge a handful side by side with the dock UI before deciding whether this layer belongs in the game.

Producing quality audio

Why the old loops failed

The rejected mining-beam and engine sounds were continuous, tonal loops tied to held keys: they played for as long as the player did the most common thing in the game, carried no new information after the first second, and could not be escaped. That is a design failure, not a failure of synthesis. Any source will annoy if it is used that way.

What a calm game needs

Layer What it does How it stays calm
Region ambienceA bed per region: the Shallows soft and glassy, Cinder Drift a low warm rumble with distant metal, Brassmouth busier with brass and radio murmur.Long loops built from several layers with different lengths so the repeat point never lines up; crossfade at lane jumps.
Station proximityNear a station its own layer fades in: greenhouse drips at Kettle Dock, lantern hum at the Wharf, coin-counting clicks at Brassmouth.Distance-based volume and a low-pass that opens as you approach; fades out once docked.
MusicSparse pieces or stems by region and act.Plays now and then with silence between pieces; never under every moment.
UISelect, confirm, cancel, trade one, trade all, open map, pause.Short, soft, low-mid wood and glass sounds, quieter than the world.
MiningA soft onset when the beam locks on, a small "chunk" tick each time a unit enters the hold, a crack when a rock breaks, a gentle cue when the hold is full.No sustained beam tone. The sound carries information (ore gained) and is otherwise near silence.
FlightA light filtered whoosh on starting and stopping.Silent while cruising; no engine loop.
DockingA two- or three-note chime in the station's motif on docking; its reverse on undocking.Musical, short, and different per station, so it is a pleasure rather than a notification.
World changeA soft motif when a chronicle entry, a new station or a faction change is revealed at docking.Only at calm moments, never during a mining run.
PauseEverything ducks and dulls under a low-pass.Silence is the feedback.

Adaptive layers follow region and activity, not time: mining adds a faint rhythmic layer that thins out the longer you mine, docking brings in station murmur, and the map view thins the bed to its pad.

Rules against fatigue

  • No loops tied to held input

    If the player holds a key for a long time, the sound for it fades to near silence after its onset.

  • Every sound carries information or is ambience

    A cue says something new (ore gained, docked, hold full). Anything else is quiet background and fades.

  • Variation on everything that repeats

    At least four to six takes per frequent cue, small random pitch and volume offsets, and never the same take twice in a row. Layering a few variations multiplies the combinations, a common practice in game sound design.

  • Rate limits

    Each cue has a cap on how often it may play; rapid repeats merge into one.

  • Soft spectrum

    Soft attacks, little energy in the harsh 2 to 5 kHz band for frequent cues, low-passed long sounds.

  • A loudness budget

    UI sits below the world, cues duck the bed only slightly, and every file is measured for peak and loudness before it ships.

  • Silence is allowed

    Music is occasional; ambience can thin to almost nothing in empty space.

  • Separate sliders

    Music, ambience, effects and UI each get a volume slider, so the player can remove whatever bothers them.

  • Judge by long listening

    Test a long play session on both headphones and laptop speakers, and keep a written list of anything that grates. That list is the real acceptance test.

Even famously procedural games mix sources: one well-known procedural space game's audio director has said that very little of its audio is procedurally created (mainly creature voices and background fauna), with the rest recorded and designed, and the music composed as material for a generative system. What kira gives us

The slice already depends on kira 0.12 (MIT or Apache-2.0). Its current API covers everything the plan above needs.

Need kira feature
Buses for music, ambience, effects and UIMixer sub-tracks: manager.add_sub_track(TrackBuilder), nestable; a track's volume affects its children; sounds play on a track.
Shared reverbSend tracks: add_send_track(SendTrackBuilder::new().with_effect(..)), fed by TrackBuilder::with_send(&send, level_db).

The audio clock is the player's real time, which is fine: audio is presentation, sits outside the deterministic sim, and only reacts to events and state, as the renderer does.

Recommendation and first steps

Rules first, then a small synthesised and CC0 cue set, music last

Build the bus layout and a cue table that enforces the anti-fatigue rules in code, then fill it with short cues synthesised offline with fundsp and a few CC0 samples, all committed to the repo. Synthesis fits agents well: Grok Build can write patches, render WAVs, measure them and lay them out on a listening page, and the owner judges by ear. Add region beds next. Leave music for last, and consider commissioning it, because it is the hardest part to get right and the easiest to grow tired of.

  1. 1
    Bus layout

    In galaxy-audio: main, then music, ambience, effects and UI sub-tracks, one reverb send, a "calm" tweener and a pause low-pass. The app still only calls methods on Audio.

  2. 2
    A cue table as data

    audio.toml: cue ID, files or patch, bus, pitch and volume ranges, no-repeat, rate limit, duck amount. Pack data, so the game master can later add rows.

  3. 3
    Five cues first

    UI confirm, docking chime, undock, ore tick and hold full. No mining loop and no engine loop.

  4. 4
    An offline render and check tool

    cargo xtask audio renders fundsp patches to WAV, measures peak, loudness, spectral centroid and length against the rules, and writes a listening page.

  5. 5
    One region bed

    The Shallows, from layered CC0 recordings and synth pads, with station proximity layers for the Wharf and Kettle Dock.

  6. 6
    A long listening test

    The owner plays with sound on and keeps the "grates" list. Nothing ships that is on it.

Assets the game master can create live

The rule: recipes in, assets out

The game master never hands the game a mesh, an image file or an audio file. It writes data: a recipe over kit parts, a palette chosen inside the rules, a decal or banner by ID, a motif as a parameter set. The base engine owns the kits, builds the asset, checks it against budgets and the art bible, renders previews the agent can look at, and caches the result by content hash. This keeps saves deterministic and small, keeps every asset on style, and makes rollback trivial: a rolled-back recipe simply stops being drawn.

    Reads history, picks a cause, writes a recipe or motif through the scripting engine.

  2. ↓ recipe as data
  3. Asset check in the script pipeline

    Schema and ranges, budgets, palette rules, placement rules.

  4. ↓
  5. Preview render Outside the tick

    Headless contact sheet: play zoom, thumbnail, silhouette, beside neighbours. Silhouette and readability scores.

    ↺ back to Game master: report and PNGs; revise and resubmit

  6. ↓ pass

    A reversible command stores the recipe; the renderer builds and caches the mesh; legibility channels name the place and the person.

  8. ↓ if it misbehaves
  9. In-world rollback Outcome

    The recipe reverts (for example to scaffold) with a dockmaster line; nothing the player owns is lost.

The asset manifest contract

Today's AssetManifest lists parts by ID with a free-form kind. To support live creation it should describe the kit precisely enough that a script, the validator and the renderer agree on what is allowed.

Section Holds Owned by
partsEach part kind: parameters with ranges and defaults, sockets, triangle cost, tags (faction motif, role: dock, living, industry, light), variants and the station stages it may appear in.Base engine (kit code), exported from Rust so it can't drift.
palettesRoles, allowed swatches, faction and region pairs, the numeric palette rules.Art bible data; factions can be recoloured live inside the rules.
decalsVector marks, stripes and numbers by ID with allowed colours and sizes.Base kit; new marks by release or reviewed pack.
motifsInstruments, scales, ranges and loudness limits for sound motifs.Base kit.
budgetsTriangles per class, parts per station, decals per part, new station types per era, motifs per region.Pack data, so pacing stays tunable.
recipesAuthored and game-master recipes by ID, with cause, stage deltas and provenance.Packs (core authored, gm live).

One structural change follows: the recipe types and validate() currently live in render, which sim and script crates may not depend on. Move the recipe data types, ranges and data checks into a render-free crate (the contract, or a small kit-spec crate) so the Luau host can validate in the tick, and keep mesh building and preview renders in render.

A proposed assets hook surface

A sketch in the style of the existing ctx.stations hooks. Every write takes a why table with the facts it cites and becomes a reversible command in the script's batch.

-- Reading the kit
ctx.assets.parts({ tag = "lamplighter" })     -- part kinds, ranges, sockets, costs
ctx.assets.palette("faction.lamplighters")    -- roles and allowed swatches
ctx.assets.decals({ faction = "faction.lamplighters" })
ctx.assets.budget("station")                  -- what is left this era

-- Dry run inside the tick: data checks only, no rendering
local report = ctx.assets.check_station(recipe)

-- Writes (each a reversible command with a cause)
ctx.assets.put_station_recipe("gm.listening_post", recipe, why)
ctx.assets.add_stage("station.kettle_dock", 4, { add = { ... } }, why)
ctx.assets.set_faction_palette("faction.tollway_compact", { primary = "#c98f4a", secondary = "#8a2f3c" }, why)
ctx.assets.put_decal("gm.listening_post", { decal = "decal.mark.open_lamp", on = "part:3", colors = { "glow", "ink" } }, why)
ctx.assets.put_banner("station.cinder_hook", { mark = "decal.mark.toll_key", palette = "faction.tollway_compact" }, why)
ctx.assets.put_motif("gm.motif.listening_post", { instrument = "glass_bell", scale = "major_pentatonic", notes = { 1, 3, 5, 3 }, register = "mid", space = 0.4 }, why)

Matching commands: PutStationRecipe, SetStationStageDelta, SetFactionPalette, PutDecal, PutMotif, AttachMotif, all reversible. The existing attach, detach and set_stage keep working for authored parts.

Validating a new asset

An "asset check" step fits into the game master's script validation pipeline right after types and schema, in two halves: data checks that run anywhere, and render checks that run outside the tick in the game-master bridge and galaxy pack check, where a GPU (or a software adapter) is available.

  1. 1
    Schema and ranges

    The recipe parses; every part kind exists; every parameter is in range (today's validate()).

  2. 2
    Budgets

    Parts, triangles, decals and reach under the class budget; new station types per era under the cap.

  3. 3
    Palette

    Every colour within a small OKLab distance of an allowed swatch; at most three hues plus ink and glow; hull lightness at least 0.25 above the sky; the owner's colour pair present.

  4. 4
    Placement

    The content spec's sixth-station rules: near a cited player fact, one at a time, a complete station with a ring and a dockmaster, the right faction.

  5. 5
    Render the contact sheet

    Play zoom, a 64-pixel thumbnail, a flat silhouette, and the asset beside its region's stations.

  6. 6
    Silhouette and readability

    Smallest feature at play zoom at least one and a half outline widths; the silhouette fills enough of its bounding circle; best-rotation overlap with any other station in the region below a threshold; the docking ring visible and reachable.

  7. 7
    Look at it

    The report carries the PNG paths. The game-master agent looks at the sheet before proposing; the owner can look at the same sheet with their own agent outside the game. There is no approval inbox in the game.

// Sketch: the render half, in the game-master bridge (not in the sim tick).
pub fn check_station_asset(recipe: &StationRecipe, cx: &AssetCheck) -> AssetReport {
    let mut r = AssetReport::new(&recipe.name);
    if let Err(errs) = recipe.validate() { r.errors.extend(errs.iter().map(|e| e.to_string())); }
    r.budget("parts", recipe.parts.len(), cx.budgets.parts);
    let mesh = kit::station(recipe);
    r.budget("triangles", mesh.triangle_count(), cx.budgets.station_triangles);
    r.palette = palette_report(&recipe.palette, &cx.art_bible, cx.region_sky);
    if !r.errors.is_empty() { return r; }               // don't render broken data

    let sil = cx.renderer.silhouette(&mesh, cx.play_view);  // flat ink on white, headless
    r.min_feature_px = sil.min_feature_px();
    r.fill = sil.fill_of_bounding_circle();
    r.max_overlap = cx.neighbours.iter()
        .map(|n| sil.best_rotation_iou(n)).fold(0.0, f32::max);
    r.ring_reachable = kit::docking_ring_center(recipe).is_some();
    r.sheet = cx.renderer.contact_sheet(&mesh, &cx.neighbours, &[
        Shot::PlayZoom, Shot::Thumb(64), Shot::Silhouette, Shot::WithNeighbours,
    ]);
    r.pass = r.errors.is_empty()
        && r.min_feature_px >= 1.5 * cx.rules.outline_px
        && r.max_overlap < cx.rules.max_silhouette_overlap
        && r.palette.ok() && r.ring_reachable;
    r
}

Generated portraits and banners

Optional, 2D only, outside the tick

A new dockmaster at the sixth station could get a portrait, and a station screen an illustrated header. These are generated by the game-master bridge, not by a Luau script, with one licence-clear model, a fixed style prompt and reference sheet. A post-process maps the image to the game palette and adds ink edges so it matches the dock UI. The file is stored with provenance (model, licence, prompt hash) and cached by hash.

Always a fallback

Every portrait slot also has a procedural version: a parametric bust from a small 2D part kit in the same palette. If generation fails, is slow, or the owner turns the layer off, the game shows the fallback and nothing breaks. Banners and marks stay vector decals from the kit rather than generated images, because they must read at play zoom and stay crisp.

Sound motifs as parameters

A motif is a short phrase (two to six notes) played by a small instrument kit: synthesised glass bell, soft pluck, warm pad, wooden knock, or a few CC0 sampled instruments. The game master picks parameters; the base engine renders it deterministically with fundsp, caches the WAV, and plays it through kira's buses.

Parameter Allowed Checked
InstrumentAn ID from the motif kitExists; suits the bus (UI, effects, music)
Scale and notesMajor or minor pentatonic, Dorian, Lydian; two to six scale stepsAll notes in the scale; no large leaps
Register and tempoLow, mid or high; a tempo range per useInside the use's range (a docking chime is short and mid)
SpaceReverb send from 0 to 1Within loudness and tail limits
IdentityAttached to a station, faction or chronicle kindInterval pattern distinct from existing motifs in the region

Rendered motifs are measured like any cue: peak, loudness, spectral centroid and length. Motifs also obey the rate limits and the "only at calm moments" rule.

Base engine or scripting layer

Three tiers rather than two: what changes only with a release, what reviewed content packs may add, and what the game master may do live.

Risks and open questions

  • The kit limits imagination Risk

    A game master can only build from the parts it has, so invented stations may start to look alike. Mitigation: grow the kit with each release, cap new station types per era, and fail recipes whose silhouette overlaps a neighbour.

  • Validators can't judge taste Risk

    Numbers catch broken and unreadable assets, not ugly ones. The contact sheet must be looked at, by the game-master agent and by the owner.

  • Licences change Risk

    AI tool terms move often, and copyright in generated output is unsettled. Keep a provenance record per file and prefer code, CC0 and authored work for anything central.

  • Public repo versus asset licences Risk

    Libraries that forbid redistributing raw files (Sonniss, Pixabay) can't live in the repo. Decide whether private builds may use them, or stay CC0-only.

  • Audio taste Risk

    Sound was rejected once already. Small sets judged by ear in long sessions, and a "grates" list, are safer than a big pass.

  • 3D later Question

    Stations are built for top-down; true-3D views need sides and undersides, and planets need their own kit. How much should be modelled now?

  • Commission a style sheet? Question

    One artist-made style sheet (palette, three station kinds, faction marks, a portrait style) would anchor agents and validators. Worth it before growing the kit?

  • Generated portraits in the game? Question

    Keep the illustrated 2D layer to authored and procedural art, or allow generated portraits with a fallback?

  • Music or not Question

    Ambience only for now, occasional generative pieces, or commissioned stems later?

  • Text on hulls Question

    Station names and pilot name plates (the Kin regard tier mentions one) are text on art. Allowed as decals, or kept in UI only?
